## The brief

A passport photograph is rejected for reasons a photographer cannot reliably see at the
moment of capture: a tilted head, a closed eye, a background that fails one country's
specification while passing another's. The application puts an AI image check between
the camera and the customer, evaluates each shot against the selected document's
requirements, and surfaces the compliant one.

It runs on an iPad in a working studio, in a photographer's hands, between a customer
sitting down and a customer leaving. Nothing in it can wait for a second look. The
compliance app is one of three products in the same suite: a self-service kiosk lets a
customer capture unassisted, and a web back-office sits behind both.

## The system

There was no system to inherit. Design started at the foundations and had to arrive as
something an implementation team could build without interpretation.

## Choosing the specification first

The document type is picked before the photograph, because it is what every later check
is run against. Each country sits behind its own flag with the specification one tap
away, and selecting a template is deliberately a separate target from asking what the
template requires.

The template picker also carries a content case rather than only ideal names: a
64-character label, wrapping to three lines and truncating, sits in the grid beside the
short ones.

## Delivering it

The photograph leaves by two routes, and both are on the same screen as the capture list
so neither is a mode you have to leave the work to reach: printed on the spot, or sent to
the customer's phone. The read-only table beside the upload states what is actually being
delivered, down to the physical and pixel size and the expiry date.

## One field, four states

The mobile number is the only free-text entry in the flow, and it is drawn four times: the
field on focus, mid-entry, complete, and re-entered with the upload still held back. The
keypad rises over the delivery panel alone, so the capture list and the preview stay on
screen throughout.

Specifying a single field this closely is what the rest of the library is built on. States
were written per component rather than left to the build.
