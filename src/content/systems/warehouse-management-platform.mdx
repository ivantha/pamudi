## The brief

An in-house warehouse management platform, built as a product to be configured for
different operations rather than forked for each one. That is the constraint that shapes
everything below: no screen can assume one customer's vocabulary, one customer's process,
or one customer's hardware.

The mobile client is used one-handed, by someone holding a scanner in the other hand,
walking. Every flow here is a loop of scan, confirm, scan again, and the design's job is
to make the loop tight enough that it does not need reading.

## Every flow starts by binding a container

Nothing is picked, sorted or counted until the app knows which physical bin the work is
going into. That step gets its own screen rather than a field on a busier one, and the
screen says what to do in its subtitle rather than in a tooltip.

## The work itself

Picking and sorting are the same shape: a list of things to work through, each carrying
its own count and its own action, with the scan field always at the foot. The pattern is
the point. Once a picker knows one module, the others do not need learning.

<Figure
  src={sortOrders}

## Numbers at the end, including the honest one

Both the pick and the count close on a summary, and both summaries report time as well
as quantity. Average time per parcel is a number that judges the operator as much as the
process, and putting it on the screen the operator sees is a deliberate choice rather
than an oversight.

## When the item will not scan

RFID search is the one screen in the set that cannot be a list, because the thing being
looked for is somewhere in a room rather than somewhere in a table. It becomes a dial: the
tag identifier at the top, signal strength as a filling arc, and start and stop as the
only two controls.
