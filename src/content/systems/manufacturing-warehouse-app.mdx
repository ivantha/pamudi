## The brief

A listed manufacturer with production sites in several countries, running one warehouse
application across manufacturing and distribution. Two clients on two very different
devices: a console for the people who configure and approve, and a phone for the people
on the floor who scan things.

The design job here was maintenance rather than authorship. The system existed; the work
was to keep it coherent while it grew across modules, and to make the two clients feel
like one product rather than two teams' output.

## The console: configuration is data entry, so make it fast

Adding a machine is a form, and the design does not pretend otherwise. What it does is
offer three routes to the same identifier: pick it, scan it, or key it. A barcode panel
sits inside the dialog rather than behind a separate scan mode, and a plain code field
sits under it for the case where the label is damaged.

## The floor: one card, reused exactly

The mobile modules could easily have drifted into three different card layouts. They do
not. A job in production and a sales order in the lab are the same card: title, status
chip, the same three labelled rows, then a three-button action bar in the same three
colours.

Quantities are bars rather than fractions because the reading is comparative, and the
number stays beside the bar for when it is not.

## Scanning, with a way out

Weight capture is the same pattern as the console's machine dialog, one screen down.
The scanner is the primary path and takes the centre of the screen, but the manual entry
underneath is not hidden behind a link. In a warehouse the label is sometimes unreadable,
and a flow that assumes otherwise stops the line.
