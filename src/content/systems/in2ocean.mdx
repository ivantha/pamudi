## What it is

Volunteers turn up to a beach cleanup, spend a morning filling bags, and go
home. IN2Ocean is the app around that morning: find an event or create one, log
what comes off the sand while you are picking it up, and finish with a number
that means something rather than a thank-you screen.

It is the one project in this inventory with no client behind it, and it is the
one where the whole surface was designed rather than maintained: identity, style
guides in both themes, illustration, and the flows below.

**What is not recorded is who did which part.** It is a group project, her own
Figma file is the only source, and no appraisal or CV mentions it. Everything on
this page describes the design; none of it apportions credit.

## The identity comes first

There is a logo guide, and it exists because the mark has to survive being small
on a phone, large on a splash screen, and reversed out on a photograph of a
beach. It carries the horizontal lockup, the stacked one, the wordmark alone,
and the palette that the rest of the product inherits.

## Collecting is counting, so make counting the screen

This is the screen the rest of the app exists to serve, and it refuses to be a
text field. Trash is a fixed vocabulary (cigarette butts, balloons, toys,
fishing gear, plastic bags, food wrappers, containers, cans, cups, plates, lids,
straws, utensils), so every one of them is a tile with its own counter, tapped
with wet hands while crouching.

What does not fit the vocabulary falls through to "Other Trash", bucketed by
size rather than by name, because a volunteer should not have to classify a
fragment to record it.

## An event is a commitment, so it reads like one

The event screen leads with the photograph and then gets specific: date, a
start and end time, a named beach, and how many people are already going. The
participant count is the persuasive part and it is stated plainly rather than
dressed up, which is the right call for something that asks for a Saturday
morning.

## Rewards that name the thing you did

Seven levels, each with its own illustration and its own sentence about what it
was for. The copy is written to the ocean rather than to the user's ego: the
level-three note thanks you on behalf of sea turtles, which is a small choice
that tells you what the product thinks it is for.

## Both themes, specified rather than derived

The style guide exists in light and dark as two full documents. Dark is not a
palette swap applied late; it is drawn out at the same length as light, which is
what makes it a specification rather than an intention.
