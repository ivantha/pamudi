## The brief

A global freight forwarder runs distribution centres in roughly thirty-nine countries.
This application detects where a load actually is inside one of them, and says so on a
screen mounted where the work happens rather than in a report someone reads later.

It is a fixed-position tablet in a warehouse, read at a distance by someone who is
holding something else. That constraint sets the type size, the colour coding and the
decision to give the running status its own permanent bar rather than a toast that
disappears while you are looking away.

## The system

There was no system to inherit. Design started at the foundations and had to arrive as
something an implementation team could build without interpretation.

## Navigation that stays put

The application has four destinations and no more: Home, Warehouse, Settings, License.
They live in a rail that expands to labels and collapses to icons, and the collapsed
state is the working default because the grid it sits beside is the actual content.

## Two ways to look at a warehouse

Racks are discrete and countable, so they are drawn as cards with a state each. A bay
is continuous floor, so it is drawn as a ruled area instead. Using one metaphor for both
would have made the second one lie.

## The failure state is a screen, not a blank

The application depends on a camera feed it does not control. When that feed drops, the
panel does not sit empty and it does not show a spinner that never resolves. It names
the fault, and puts the one action that fixes it in the middle of the space the video
would have filled.
