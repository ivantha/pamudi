## The brief

Tools and consumables are lent out of relocatable container stores parked on mining,
energy and construction sites. The sites are remote, the stores are unstaffed for much
of the day, and the people using them are wearing gloves. The platform tracks what left,
what came back, and what needs servicing, across web, mobile and an on-site kiosk.

Three clients, one system. That is the whole design problem: a console used at a desk, a
handheld used in a yard, and a kiosk used at a container door in daylight all have to be
recognisably the same product without pretending they are the same device.

## The rail is the product

Issue, Return, Maintenance. Three verbs, drawn as large icon targets in a permanent left
rail, with the signed-in user pinned above them and Logout pinned below. Nothing about
the current screen can move them, because on a kiosk at a container door the person in
front of it may not be the person who started the session.

## Two levels of "what is this"

The catalogue separates the type of thing from the individual thing, and the console
keeps that distinction visible as a pair of tabs rather than burying it in a form. A
resin disc is an object type; the four in the container are items.

That is also why adding a type is a heavier dialog than adding an item. It defines a
taxonomy that everything below it inherits.

## Designed for the connection it will not have

A container store on a remote site loses its link regularly. The offline state is
therefore a designed screen with the module's own illustration, not a browser error page,
and it says what the user should conclude ("you are in offline now") rather than only
what failed.
